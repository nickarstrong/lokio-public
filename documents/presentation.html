Speaker notes

This is LOKIO. A new format of social interaction tied to a place rather than a feed. The product is live at app.lokio.org. You can open it on any device while we talk.

Two examples of what gets posted on LOKIO. Left: a need (someone asks for company at the airport). Right: a self-deprecating thought from a bench, the kind of post that is the daily reason people open the app. The product handles both registers from the same single text field.

The entire mechanic is this flow. Open the map of your city. Type. Publish. The thought appears at your location, visible to people physically nearby. They reply, conversation starts, you meet. Five seconds from opening to publishing. Everything else in the product is downstream of this.

This is the kind of moment LOKIO solves that no other product touches cleanly. A traveller alone in Istanbul, hotel in Sultanahmet. They open LOKIO, see pins around the hotel, find someone with a free seat at a restaurant table, tap join. No dating profile. No event creation. No "looking for friends" forum. A pin and a tap.

The most underestimated part of the product is psychological. Every existing alternative requires something of the user before letting them in. A declaration of intent for Tinder. Responsibility to organise an event for Meetup. A content plan for Instagram. The median person is not willing to do any of those things. They will never use these products. LOKIO requires nothing. That's why it works for them. This is product-market fit at the level of psychology.

Coffee posts and meet-ups happen when a user has a need. That is episodic. The daily reason people open LOKIO is humour. Self-deprecating thoughts from a bench. Recognition. People who pass that intersection daily nod and reply with their own version. A district develops a shared voice. Twitter is global, so local memes have no surface today. LOKIO is that surface, and it is what brings users back when they don't need anything specifically.

The flywheel is what people do without our intervention. A user came in to drop a thought, did not expect a reply, got one. From there: friendships, partners, businesses, communities, all emerging without product features added. We did not build dating, we did not build events, we did not build a marketplace. We built a single primitive (a thought tied to a place) and people found these uses themselves.

Everything in the previous slides exists in software you can open right now at app.lokio.org. Authentication, real-time map, chat, public profiles, all live. The 22+ organic topics that have appeared on it. We frame them honestly as an early propagation signal, not as traction. We have spent $0 on advertising, outreach, or marketing. The product is open and verifiable in this room while we talk.

This is the economic case for the business side of LOKIO. A small café in the existing model spends $3,500 a month (marketer, ads, SEO) and the result is unknown until the month closes. On LOKIO the same café publishes a banner pin for $4.99, geo-bounded to the district, surfaces on the free-coffee filter for users actively looking for offers, ROI measurable on the same day. Google Ads, Facebook Ads. Neither is local-bounded the way LOKIO is. The economics are not comparable.

Pricing is not static. A new city has zero supply, charging $4.99 on day one would kill adoption. So prices ramp with city density. Tier 0 is new city, everything free. Tier 1 is first publishers, prices at 30%. Tier 2 is district density, 60%. Tier 3 is city has crossed threshold, full prices. The coefficient is automatic, not human-set. The ramp is one-way: once a city moves up, prices do not come back down. Same logic Uber used with driver subsidies, automated. No quarterly business review per market. The system runs itself.

Per city of 10,000 monthly active users at tier 3, costs about $300/month, revenue $1,100 to $1,950, margin 70 to 85%. Margin is high because user acquisition cost trends to zero. The product explains itself, no convincing required. Scaling: ten cities of that size, $234k annualised. A single 100k-MAU city, also $234k. Ten such cities, $2.3M. We grow city by city, like Uber, not winner-takes-all.

Here is a fact most people do not have at hand. China's GDP per capita in 1978 was $156. In 2022 it was $12,663. An 81-fold increase in 44 years. The largest sustained per-capita growth in recorded history of any large economy. The next slide shows what drove it. And why this is directly relevant to the product we are talking about.

What drove China's growth was the layer of small and medium enterprises. Today they make up 98.5% of all enterprises in the country. They produce 60% of GDP, 80% of urban employment, 50% of tax revenue, 70% of patents. 140 million businesses. Alibaba's first product in 1999 was a marketplace for small Chinese exporters. Tencent's WeChat Pay turned every street vendor into a digital business. The trillion-dollar names that emerged from China started as infrastructure for small business, not as alternatives to it.

LOKIO mechanically sits on the small business side of the divide. A café reaches its district for $4.99. A solo trainer fills a class with one map post. A neighbourhood market announces fresh stock without a marketer. A street vendor tells the street where they are today. None of this scales for chains because chains do not need geo-bounded posts. They own broadcast advertising. Every paid post on LOKIO is, by structural design, an act of small commerce.
